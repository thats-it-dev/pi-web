# Keep the 27B planner, drop FP8 to NVFP4

No model that fits beside the resident Qwen3.6-35B-A3B plans better than Qwen3.8-27B, so the realistic win is precision rather than architecture: the current orchestrator is slow mainly because the pi fine-tune is served as a **30.9 GB FP8** checkpoint on a 273 GB/s box, which caps plain decode near 8-9 tok/s and leaves DFlash2 multiplying a small base, while the same model at **NVFP4 (~19-22 GB)** has a 14.5 tok/s plain ceiling and reaches 48-65 tok/s on code with DFlash2 on SGLang ([ai-muninn](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-bandwidth-ceiling-85-percent)). Your own 27-31 tok/s for base NVFP4 versus 20.8 for pi FP8 is exactly the 1.3-1.6x the weight-byte ratio predicts, so the recommendation is to quantize bytkim's pi tune to NVFP4 locally (no community NVFP4 pi build exists), keep SGLang + DFlash2, and expect ~27-31 tok/s at one stream and ~12-14 tok/s while four 35B workers decode (estimate, scaled from your 9.2/20.8 ratio). Among other models, only Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Super VL combine stronger-or-comparable agentic scores with higher speed, and both need essentially the whole box, so they are either/or replacements for the pair, not orchestrators beside it; Nemotron 3.5 Bolt fits easily and runs 2-3x faster but scores a tier lower (Terminal-Bench 2.1 51 vs 73, vendor numbers) with 2-bit experts; gpt-oss-120b, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B, GLM-4.5-Air, Gemma 4 and Mistral Small 4 are each slower, weaker, or both. One hypothesis is worth a half-hour check before anything else: your DFlash2 acceptance of 3.5 on agent traffic is low compared with the 4.8-6.1 reported for the base model on code, and the incoai draft was trained against base Qwen3.8-27B, so the fine-tune may be costing acceptance on top of FP8 costing bandwidth.

## FP8 weights, not planning, explain the 20.8 tok/s

Decode on the GB10 is bandwidth-bound and the community measurements fit a simple model: single-stream speed is 60-90% of 273 GB/s divided by the bytes of weights read per token. The most careful single-model study puts the Qwen3.8-27B NVFP4 language backbone at **18.77 GB, a 14.5 tok/s ceiling, and 12.32 tok/s measured** on SGLang (85% of ceiling, 10 runs, 0.67% spread) ([ai-muninn part 42](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-bandwidth-ceiling-85-percent)). The same dense model on vLLM at FP8 (30.9 GB) decodes at **7.9 tok/s** plain and 17.1 with MTP-3, versus 9.7 and 18.5 at NVFP4; at BF16 (55.6 GB) it is 4.5 tok/s ([CordatusAI study, NVIDIA forums](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/comprehensive-qwen3-8-27b-study-on-dgx-sparks-quantization-speculative-decoding-and-tp-dp-scaling/381102)). Speculative decoding scales those bases: DFlash2 with an 8-token draft budget takes NVFP4 from 12.3 to **53.9 tok/s on code, 28.1 on prose and 26.0 on chat** with acceptance length 6.12; budget 12 reaches 65 on code; MTP-5 gives 35.7 and DSpark 42.9 ([ai-muninn part 42](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-bandwidth-ceiling-85-percent)). A second SGLang DFlash2 recipe reports 47.9 tok/s at one stream on NVFP4 and QAI measured 25-41 tok/s depending on task type, with per-pass acceptance of 3.8-5.0 ([OpenZeka](https://blog.openzeka.com/en/?p=1977); [QAI](https://qai.io/blog/dgx-spark-qwen3.8-27b-vllm-vs-sglang)). The one FP8 + DFlash2 data point in the notes, 31.7 tok/s on vLLM, comes from a single poster and is cited second-hand ([QAI](https://qai.io/blog/dgx-spark-qwen3.8-27b-vllm-vs-sglang)).

Your numbers sit where that model says they should. FP8 reads 1.64x the bytes of NVFP4 per verify step, so an FP8 orchestrator at acceptance 3.5 landing at 20.8 tok/s against 27-31 for base NVFP4 on the same box is the precision penalty, not a planning-model limitation. Agent traffic is the worst case for drafting: plans, specs and tool-call JSON accept less than code, and the DSpark recipe author saw a **56K-context agentic session at 18-23 tok/s** against 31-47 on code and math ([NVIDIA forums, 34-38 tok/s thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/qwen3-8-27b-at-34-38-tok-s-on-dgx-spark-open-source-one-command-setup-sglang-nvfp4-dspark/380257)). The 9.2 tok/s under four workers is bandwidth contention between two engines, which no source measures directly (the notes flag it as a gap); the arithmetic says four 35B-A3B streams activate extra experts and read roughly 4-5 GB per step, consuming perhaps half the bus, which is consistent with your 0.44 ratio. Any orchestrator suffers proportionally, so halving its weight bytes helps under load as much as it helps alone, and the pair design already hides most of the problem because a dispatching orchestrator is idle while workers run; the 9.2 only bites during async reviews or overlapping turns.

The acceptance length deserves its own test. Reported DFlash2 acceptance on base Qwen3.8-27B is 4.39-5.46 on the draft's own evals (HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K) and 6.1-8.1 on Spark code runs ([incoai/Qwen3.8-27B-DFlash2](https://huggingface.co/incoai/Qwen3.8-27B-DFlash2); [ai-muninn part 42](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-bandwidth-ceiling-85-percent)). The draft "supports only Qwen3.8-27B as target" and bytkim ships it unchanged, mirrored from Inco AI, with no note on acceptance against the tuned weights ([bytkim/Qwen3.8-27B-pi](https://huggingface.co/bytkim/Qwen3.8-27B-pi)). A GRPO reasoning-efficiency reward shifts the token distribution exactly where the drafter predicts it, so acceptance of 3.5 could be partly fine-tune mismatch and partly prose-heavy agent traffic; only measuring base versus pi on identical prompts separates the two.

## Nothing that fits beside the 35B plans better than a dense 27B

On the evidence available, the dense Qwen3.8-27B is the strongest planner in the single-Spark class. Its card reports **Terminal-Bench 2.1 (Terminus) 73.0, SWE-bench Pro 61.7, IFBench 79.5 and Agents' Last Exam 20.4**, versus 63.4 / 53.5 / 69.1 / 10.6 for Qwen3.6-27B, all vendor-reported with a Claude Code harness at temperature 1.0 ([Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B)). Third-party Terminal-Bench 2.0 ranks the same-generation dense model above both Qwen MoEs: **Qwen3.6-27B 0.593, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B 0.515, Qwen3.5-122B-A10B 0.494**, and gpt-oss-120b at 0.191 ([llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/terminal-bench-2); [Benchmarklist](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/vals_terminal_bench_2/)). The 10-12B-active MoEs do not reverse that: Nemotron 3 Super 120B-A12B scores SWE-bench (OpenHands) 60.5, Terminal-Bench core 31.0 and tau2 average 61.2, and NVIDIA's own table shows it trailing Qwen3.5-122B-A10B on every SWE harness ([Nemotron 3 Super report](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.12374)). Within one vendor's harness the pattern is that same-generation dense 27B beats small-active MoE by 8-12 points on long-horizon work (TB2, SWE-bench Pro, NL2Repo) while single-call tool scores (TAU3, MCP-Atlas) stay level ([Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B)).

Two measured results say the manager seat is where to spend the slow tokens. In Multi-Agent Computer Use, with a Qwen3.5-4B worker fixed, **Qwen3.6-27B as manager reached 41.7%**, level with Gemini 3.1 Pro and Kimi K2.6, against 25.0% for the worker alone and 58.3% under Opus 4.6 ([arXiv 2606.01533](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01533)). OrchBench finds planner quality is mostly "transfer coverage", routing the right information between agents, and places Qwen3.6-35B-A3B mid-pack at 0.768/0.694/0.595 for 10/20/50 tasks, within ~0.05 of frontier planners ([arXiv 2607.25656](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.25656v1)). The one practitioner head-to-head (anecdote, n=3 questions, a pi-style harness on an L4) found Qwen3.8-27B and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B gave similar answers, but the dense model made fewer, better tool calls and sometimes won wall-clock despite 4x slower decode ([Han Xiao, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hxiao87_if-you-are-building-agentic-search-with-minimum-activity-7494914008748093440-e3Ld)). That matters for the "switch to base NVFP4" option: the pi tune's only claimed gain is token efficiency, **medium effort matching base xhigh completion on a TB2.1 subset with ~41% fewer output tokens** (fine-tuner's own charts, single seed) ([bytkim/Qwen3.8-27B-pi-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/bytkim/Qwen3.8-27B-pi-GGUF); [aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/qwen38-27b-pi-matches-base-xhigh-accuracy-with-41-fewer-tokens)). If that claim holds, pi FP8 at 20.8 tok/s already beats base NVFP4 at 27-31 on wall-clock per task (20.8/0.59 ≈ 35 equivalent), which is why the answer is pi-at-NVFP4 rather than base-at-NVFP4. If it does not hold on your tasks, base NVFP4 is the simpler path and you lose nothing on peak quality, since the card does not claim higher accuracy.

The non-Qwen field has one real challenger on paper. **Nemotron 3.5 Super VL 120B-A12B** (Oct 2026, OpenMDW-1.1) reports SWE-bench Verified 71.1, SWE-bench Pro 51.8, Terminal-Bench 2.1 60.7 (NVFP4 62.8) and tau2 retail/telecom 69/73, all vendor numbers with no independent replication yet ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL-120B-A12B-NVFP4)); note that NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 training data included SWE-bench Verified itself, which argues for discounting the family's SWE numbers ([Nemotron 3 Super card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B-BF16)). Its sibling **Nemotron 3.5 Bolt 74B-A9B** ships natively at 2-bit experts in 22 GB and scores Terminal-Bench 2.1 51.0, MCP-Atlas 52.2 and BrowseComp 66.8 against 44.4 / 51.2 / 48.7 for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, with no SWE-bench Verified published ([Bolt report](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33039v1)). Both trail the 27B's 73.0 on the same benchmark name, with the caveat that harnesses differ across vendors. gpt-oss-120b sits a generation behind on long-horizon work (Terminal-Bench 2.0 ~19%, SWE-bench Verified 62.4 only at high effort) and generated ~25.6 Harmony-format errors per instance in one repo-level study ([gpt-oss card](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10925); [Vals TB2](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/terminal-bench-2); [arXiv 2603.25770](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25770)). GLM-4.5-Air (SWE-V 57.6, Terminal-Bench 30), Gemma 4 26B-A4B (SWE-bench Verified 17.4 on Qwen's table, infinite tool-call loops in OpenCode) and Mistral Small 4 (Terminal-Bench 2.0 ~17%, AA index 4) are not orchestrator material ([llm-stats GLM](https://llm-stats.com/models/compare/glm-4.5-vs-glm-4.5-air); [opencode #21034](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode/issues/21034); [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/mistral-small-4)).

Second, the NVFP4 flavour matters: the fast single-stream path on GB10 is W4A16 (NVFP4 storage, BF16 compute), which beat W4A4 FP4 tensor cores 74.8 vs 58.3 tok/s at batch 1 on a 30B-A3B, and quants that keep the lm_head in BF16 (RadixArk's NVFP4-BF16-LMHead, 21.9 GB) are what the DFlash2 recipes use ([ai-muninn part 25](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-nemotron-3-nano-w4a16-74-toks); [CordatusAI study](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/comprehensive-qwen3-8-27b-study-on-dgx-sparks-quantization-speculative-decoding-and-tp-dp-scaling/381102)). Practitioners warn the 27B degrades at Q4 GGUF for coding (anecdotes in the LinkedIn thread and developersdigest), but the NVFP4 runs above report no quality complaints and a two-Spark DFlash2 test matched greedy baseline output on 6/6 prompts ([HF cfontes/qwen-dflash2-spark](https://huggingface.co/cfontes/qwen-dflash2-spark)); DFlash2 itself is not bit-exact despite the card's claim (1/20 prompts identical at temp 0 in one audit, quality preserved) ([GLM SGLang post](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/glm-5-3-flash-dflash2-on-sglang-2x-dgx-spark-first-sglang-path-recipe-4-gb10-fixes-honest-numbers-concurrency-curve/381703)).

## Flash-Next and Super VL only work as replacements for the pair

Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is the strongest planner Qwen has open-weighted below the 2.4T model, and its vendor card beats the 27B on the agentic rows that exist for both (DeepSWE 58.7 vs 42.2, Toolathlon 73.5 vs 67.1, SWE-bench Multilingual 81.0 vs 73.8, IFBench 81.3 vs 79.5), though Terminal-Bench 2.1 is unpublished for it ([Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next)). The problem is the 51 GB n-gram table: the RadixArk NVFP4 build is 126 GiB and the SGLang cookbook says a single Spark "does not fit in memory" without file-backing the table on NVMe, at **27.5 tok/s with MTP and 15.9 without** ([SGLang cookbook](https://docs.sglang.io/cookbook/autoregressive/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next)). The best vLLM recipe needs ~100 GiB free, streams the table with 14 patches and reaches 67.5-69.3 tok/s median (prose 38-41, coding 70-74), and NVIDIA's own FP4-Local checkpoint uses 70.3 GiB of model plus 23.2 GiB of KV at util 0.80 for ~49 tok/s at zero depth falling to ~33 at 32K ([six-weeks thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/six-weeks-optimizing-qwen3-8-flash-next-on-one-dgx-spark-67-5-tok-s-median-decode-80-on-structured-work-reproducible-recipe-with-raw-data/385463); [FP4-Local thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/nvidia-official-qwen3-8-flash-next-checkpoint-for-single-dgx-spark/385994)). Nothing is left for a 22 GB subagent plus its KV pool, and the sources say unified-memory exhaustion on this model "can require a power cycle". As an either/or it has a real case: one 6B-active model serving orchestrator and workers decodes at 26.4 per stream across 4 streams and 21.7 across 6, better than your 9.2 under load ([ai-muninn part 45](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/qwen38-flash-next-nvfp4-dgx-spark-vllm-recipe)). Against that: the thinking + tools "!!!!" loop on sm_121 was fixed upstream only on 25 Sep 2026 with intermittent loop and early-termination reports persisting into October, several Spark recipes still run thinking off (which forfeits the planner's reasoning), boot takes 10-55 minutes, and the Qwen Community License requires a separate licence for an "AI Work Assistant" product, with internal use exempt ([sglang #36537](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/issues/36537); [LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next/blob/main/LICENSE)). Choosing it means giving up the pair design and the pi tune, not adding a faster orchestrator to it.

Nemotron 3.5 Super VL has the same shape of problem with less evidence. Its NVFP4 footprint is inferred at 75-80 GB from the identically shaped Nemotron 3 Super checkpoint (75.0 GB safetensors, ~77 GB at runtime, MTP adding 20 GB and OOMing a 96 GB card), which puts it at ~100 GB with the subagent before any KV ([vLLM recipe](https://recipes.vllm.ai/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B-BF16); [HF discussion #9](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B-NVFP4/discussions/9)). The hybrid-Mamba KV is small (vLLM measured <5% KV use single-user on Nemotron 3 Super), so a 128K orchestrator context would cost little, but the only Spark run of the 3.5 model OOM'd one of its c4 trials on an otherwise empty box and its speed is reported as 43 (independent llama-benchy), ~70 (author) and ~26 (a tracker), a spread that itself says the recipe is immature ([NVIDIA forums](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/nemotron-3-5-bolt-and-super-vl-on-one-spark-with-one-command/386093); [canitrun](https://canitrun.dev/gpus/dgx-spark/)). It is the model to re-check in a few months if an independent Terminal-Bench run and a measured co-residency appear.

Bolt is the one non-Qwen model that changes the economics today. At 22.0 GB of weights (23.8 GB with runtime state and the 1.13B LiLiCorr drafter, acceptance ~4.18) it leaves ~75 GB beside the subagent, and independent llama-benchy on one Spark shows **70.8 tok/s at one stream, ~56 at 32K depth**, with NVIDIA quoting 84 ([Bolt report](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.33039v1); [forum thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/nemotron-3-5-bolt-and-super-vl-on-one-spark-with-one-command/386093)). The cost is quality: Terminal-Bench 2.1 51.0 against the 27B's 73.0 (both vendor, different harnesses), no SWE-bench published, IQ2_XXS experts that are a review-and-judgement risk, and a 3-bit variant that OOM'd in one laptop test. It belongs in the verification plan as a control, not as the default: if your tasks are such that a 51-class planner at 70 tok/s beats a 73-class planner at 30, the pair's bottleneck is wall-clock rather than judgement, and that is cheap to find out.

Memory for rank 1 is comfortable and stays that way with worker KV. The 27B at NVFP4 with fp8 KV and mem-fraction 0.50 on SGLang left **52.7 GB free after startup with 262K context**, with weights 22.1 GB, draft 3.4 GB and Mamba caches ~12.2 GB ([OpenZeka](https://blog.openzeka.com/en/?p=1977)); the 35B-A3B KV costs roughly 10 KB/token (derived from a 7.1M-token pool at util 0.80), so four 150K worker contexts are ~6 GB ([Qwen3.6-35B thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/benchmark-report-qwen3-6-35b-a3b-nvfp4-on-nvidia-dgx-spark-jetson-thor-blackwell-6000-pro/371810)). Keep the orchestrator's SGLang fraction at 0.50 or below, drop page cache before launch, and keep both engines' fractions plus ~5 GB each under 0.90 of the pool; SGLang at 0.95 with concurrent requests caused hard reboots and vLLM at ≥0.87 swapped ([ai-muninn part 42](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-bandwidth-ceiling-85-percent); [DFlash 122B post](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/dflash-for-qwen3-5-122b-a10b-80-tok-s-on-1x-spark/374328)).

## Verify in an afternoon: acceptance first, then an NVFP4 pi build

The cheapest check needs no new weights. Serve base Qwen3.8-27B NVFP4 and pi FP8 under the identical SGLang + DFlash2 recipe and replay 10-20 real orchestrator turns (spec writing, plan, dispatch JSON, a review) at medium effort, logging tok/s and per-pass acceptance from SGLang's speculative stats; also sweep `--speculative-num-draft-tokens` 6/8/12 on the pi model, since larger budgets raised code throughput but cut chat to 23 tok/s at 16 ([ai-muninn part 42](https://ai-muninn.com/en/blog/dgx-spark-bandwidth-ceiling-85-percent)). If base and pi accept within ~0.5 of each other, the gap is purely FP8 and the NVFP4 build will recover the full 1.3-1.6x; if pi accepts materially lower, part of the remedy is a drafter re-fit or a smaller budget, and the NVFP4 gain will be smaller than the base measurement suggests. Pin the model name in every run, since pi test runs that fall back to the 27B unload the other model and stall the hub (your own note from earlier work).

The NVFP4 build is the second step and the one that needs care, because no community NVFP4 of the pi tune exists and the FP8 pi checkpoint cannot be requantized without going through BF16 ([bytkim profile](https://huggingface.co/bytkim)). Quantize `bytkim/Qwen3.8-27B-pi` (BF16) to NVFP4 with the same recipe RadixArk used (NVFP4 MLP and attention, BF16 lm_head), calibrating on a few hundred of your own pi session transcripts, which bytkim says is how the GGUF quants were calibrated; keep the DFlash2 draft and fp8 KV, mem-fraction 0.50, budget 8. Three measurements settle it: single-stream tok/s and acceptance on the same replayed turns (target ≥27), tok/s while four 35B workers decode a fixed script (target ≥12), and a quality gate of 8-10 short pi tasks from your local benchmark set at medium effort scored on completion, output tokens and wall-clock against pi FP8 and base NVFP4, with a greedy agreement spot-check on 5 prompts between pi FP8 and pi NVFP4 as a quantization sanity test. Expect NVFP4 to lose nothing visible if the calibration set is representative; the published 27B NVFP4 runs show no degradation reports, but your coding tasks are the only test that counts and a 27B at 4-bit GGUF has drawn complaints for coding (anecdotes).

Run Bolt as a control for one hour if you want the counterfactual on the table: it loads in llama.cpp with its drafter in a single command and 22 GB, needs no quantization work, and its 70 tok/s against the 27B's ~30 on the same 8-10 tasks tells you whether planning quality or decode speed is the pair's real constraint. Do not spend time on Flash-Next beside the 35B, on Nemotron 3.5 Super VL until its footprint beside a second engine is measured by someone, or on gpt-oss-120b, GLM-4.5-Air, Gemma 4, Mistral Small 4 or Qwen3.5-122B for this role; each is dominated on quality, fit or both by a candidate above it.

## Conclusion

The question assumed a model problem and the evidence points at a serving one: a dense 27B at FP8 on a 273 GB/s bus can never exceed ~9 tok/s before drafting, and every orchestrator that scores higher on long-horizon benchmarks is either too big to sit beside the subagent or has not been measured in that configuration. The pair design holds up well under the published orchestration research, which rewards the manager's judgement and information routing more than its speed, so the 27B stays and sheds a third of its bytes.

The open items that would change this ranking are specific and checkable: an independent Terminal-Bench 2.1 and a measured co-resident footprint for Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, a DFlash2 drafter re-fit for the pi tune if acceptance proves to be the hidden cost, and any first-party measurement of two engines decoding on one GB10, which no source in this research provides and which your 9.2 tok/s number currently stands in for.
